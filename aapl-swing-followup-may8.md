## Part 3 — Why the 52-Week High Rejection Matters

Three things have to happen for this to be a significant warning:
1. ✅ Price reaches a major resistance level (52W high = no overhead supply above, but also psychological "sell the new high" behavior)
2. ✅ Price reverses sharply from that level intraday
3. ✅ Volume is *below* average — meaning buyers didn't show up in force to defend the new high

All three happened today.

When a stock hits a 52-week high and *can't close near it* on a day with lower-than-average volume, it tells you:
- The buyers who drove the breakout are not being replaced by new buyers
- The sellers stepped in at the new high and overwhelmed demand
- The move may be exhausted at this level

> **This does not mean AAPL is crashing. It means the probability of a mean-reversion pullback just went up meaningfully.**

## Part 7 — The Deeper Lesson: New Highs Are Not Automatic Entries

Most retail traders see "new 52-week high" and think: *momentum, breakout, buy.*

Professional swing traders see "new 52-week high on below-average volume with a reversal candle" and think: *exhaustion signal, wait for confirmation.*

The difference is reading the **character** of the price action, not just the direction.

**The four-part test for a valid 52W high breakout:**
1. Price closes near the high (not with a long upper wick) ❌ — today closed $4.69 off the high
2. Volume expands above average ❌ — 40.41M vs 44.25M avg
3. The prior day's candle supports the breakout (bullish) — needs review
4. Price holds above the prior day's high in the next session — unknown yet

Today failed tests 1 and 2. That's not confirmation of a breakout — it's a warning that the breakout may not hold.

> **"A new 52-week high is a location. A new 52-week high that holds with volume is a breakout. These are different things."**
